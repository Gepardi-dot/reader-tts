Design the Storybook Reader provider lab as a premium desktop web application screen. This page should feel like a cozy narration room built into a private archive: part casting room, part listening nook, part performance tuning bench. It should turn the technical act of choosing a TTS provider and voice into a composed, tactile, high-trust experience rather than a generic settings form.

**DESIGN SYSTEM (REQUIRED):**
- Platform: Web, desktop-first with responsive mobile fallback
- Mood: Architectural personal archive, honey-oak bookshelf wall, tactile, warm, literary, calm
- Background: Honey Oak Base (#FFF8F5, #FFEADC, #FFF1E9) with Warm Timber Shadow accents (#A98467, #82756A, #442B14) for depth and shelving structure
- Surface Light: Parchment Insert (#FFDCC2) for slips, ledger cards, notes, and paper-facing content
- Primary Accent: Brass Signal family with plaque tones around #805600, #D9A34B, and #F6BD62 for engraved actions and highlighted reading controls
- Secondary Accent: Oxide Rose (#C1666B to #97454A) for notes, highlights, and annotation warmth
- Supporting Accent: Alpine Teal (#4A7C7A to #346665) for playback readiness, provider metadata, and technical confidence
- Text Primary: Warm timber-dark text (#2C1603 / #442B14) on light surfaces; reserve dark backgrounds for small anchored areas only
- Text Secondary: Warm catalog tones, never cold gray
- Typography: Newsreader for headlines and literary body, Work Sans for labels and metadata
- Bookshelves: Straight-on shelf wall, visible plank thickness, multiple rows, front-facing covers, and tighter rhythmic spacing
- Buttons: Brass plaques or engraved rectangular controls with only slight rounding, no pill-heavy UI
- Panels: Parchment slips, ledger cards, shelf tabs, and inset trays instead of generic app modules
- Inputs: Understated and embedded, as if part of a catalog station or reading ledge
- Layout: One featured narration nook inside a larger architectural shelf wall with dense but curated shelf rhythm
- Composition Rule: Replace empty dark luxury space with warm structured shelving and integrated paper details
- Shape Rule: Prefer square or lightly softened corners; surfaces should feel architectural, framed, or paper-cut
- Viewport Rule: This screen must fit within a single premium desktop viewport with no vertical scrolling; all important controls and choices must be visible immediately

**PAGE STRUCTURE:**
1. **Top navigation:** Product identity, active book or chapter context, a compact render status chip, and a primary action to test or export the selected voice.
2. **Hero comparison zone:** A large central composition that compares two or three voice/provider candidates with clear emphasis on the currently selected option, including tonal descriptors, latency, quality, and a visible waveform or playback state.
3. **Voice cast panel:** A refined list or card rail of voices that feels curated, almost like casting roles for narration instead of browsing raw dropdown options.
4. **Provider diagnostics:** A beautiful technical section for engine, cost tier, latency, language support, chapter fit, and output format, presented like archival plaques or shelf labels using restrained teal accents.
5. **Prompting and style controls:** A deliberate control deck for narration style, pacing, pronunciation or emphasis presets, and test sample actions, integrated as a catalog station or listening cabinet rather than a plain settings stack.
6. **Trust details:** Include enough metadata to imply real product depth, such as queue position, last render time, estimated export duration, and fallback behavior.

**IMPORTANT NOTES:**
- This is not a generic admin settings page.
- Make the provider choice feel curated and confidence-building, not merely technical.
- Keep one obvious focal composition instead of flattening everything into repeated form controls.
- Preserve the accent colors, but place them inside the new honey-oak, parchment, and shelf-wall world.
- The page should feel like a premium narration lab for serious readers, housed inside an elegant archive wing rather than a software dashboard.
- Do not design a long page. Keep everything important above the fold in one composed screen.
